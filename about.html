About

Telos Research Group helps companies build fleets of coding agents that can be trusted with real systems.

Most teams putting agents on their codebase end up choosing between two bad options. Let the agent work unsupervised, and you are merging a diff nobody watched get written. Review every change by hand, and you have capped the whole thing at the throughput of one reader — which is the speed you already had.

There is a third option, and it is structural rather than clever: decide once what shape a change has to take, and make that shape hold when nobody is watching. Approval happens while a decision is still cheap to reverse. Verification is done by something with no stake in the result. Completion is demonstrated rather than reported. How it works makes the case in full.

That structure is what we build, adapt, and hand over — along with the judgment about where it earns its cost and where it plainly does not. A firm that sells you orchestration for every change is selling you overhead.

Code is the working reference implementation: open and readable, rather than described in a slide.
